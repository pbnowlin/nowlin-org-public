  • The Appeal to Common Sense — Rom 6:16
    • Paul's reasoning, persuading, apologetic approach
    • The terms
      • Know ye not — a favorite expression
      • Yield, present, hand yourself over
      • Servants — slaves
        • Servant — voluntary
        • Slave — not voluntary
        • We are a slave to sin or a slave to grace
      • Sin versus obedience — not faith
        • Sin unto death
        • Obedience unto righteousness, not unto life
      • Not being under law is not being lawless, it is being under obedience
    • The propositions
      • If we present ourselves as slaves to any power, we become the slaves of that power
      • There are only two ultimate powers
        • One is sin — in Adam
        • The other is obedience to God — in Christ
      • These two powers, sin and obedience, are entirely different and absolutely opposed to each other
        • Sin, Satan, Hell, Rebellion, Death
        • Obedience, God, Heaven, Holiness, Righteousness
      • It is impossible to be slaves to both masters at the same time, they oppose each other — Mat 6:24
      • We proclaim what our position is and who our master is by what we do — Mat 7:15-23
        • Profession is not authoritative
          • If a man continues in sin he is not under grace
          • Whoever continues in sin — is the slave of sin
        • Obedience is authoritative
          • To be a Christian is not merely saying so
          • It means we are born again
            • We are in Christ
            • We have a new nature
            • The new nature expresses itself
              • In obedience
              • In righteousness
              • In holy living
            • The slave owner insists upon a certain type of conduct; therefore if you look at a man's conduct you can tell who is his master
        • The Scripture is authoritative — Jhn 8:32-45, 1Jo 1:6, 1Jo 2:4
        • The test — what is being produced through our life
          • Slavery to sin produces sin
          • Slavery to obedience produces righteousness
            • The person who has been justified by faith through the grace of God has a power in him that produces good fruit. It is inside — in the man
