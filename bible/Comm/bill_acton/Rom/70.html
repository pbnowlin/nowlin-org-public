  • The Anticipated Question — "Is God Unjust, Unfair, or Unrighteous?"
  • The Dogmatic Answer "Absolutely Not!"
  • God's Declaration to Moses
    • God says, "I will have mercy (compassion) on whom I will have mercy (compassion)."
    • It is His sovereign right
    • It is His decree
  • The Biblical Summary
    • The exercise of mercy and compassion — not in man —will
    • The exercise of mercy and compassion — not in man —effort
    • The Biblical fact
      • Not all are elect
      • Not all are loved
      • Not all are given mercy and compassion
  • The Way that God Used Pharaoh — Rom 9:17-18
    • God exercised His sovereign predestination with Pharaoh
    • God is not unjust with Pharaoh
    • God is sovereign in exercising mercy and hardening hearts
    • God hardened Pharaoh —heart " Exo 4:21, Rom 9
    • Pharaoh represents all the wicked
    • God hardened Pharaoh —heart so that Pharaoh would rebel against God
    • It is God's right to use Pharaoh for His own purposes
    • God raised Pharaoh up
      • To become the King of Egypt
      • To serve His purpose through rebellion
    • Pharaoh is a predestinated vessel of wrath fitted for destruction
  • The Great Purpose for God Using Pharaoh
    • To reveal who He is to man — "My Name"
    • To reveal His power to subdue evil
      • The wicked are His enemies
      • They must be destroyed
  • God's Enemies and Our Enemies Do Not Stand in Their Own Power
  • God Works in the Lives of the Elect and the Non-Elect
    • To the elect He causes His Grace to enter
      • Repentance
      • Faith
      • Response
    • To the others He exercises His wrath and condemnation and hardens their heart in rebellion against Him
  • How the Lord Hardens Men and Yet Holds Them Responsible
    and Is Himself Free from Sin "
    • Cannot fully understand because we are finite and He is infinite
    • Nevertheless that fact stands on the basis of His own Word
  • The Process of God's Work in Pharaoh
    • God made His will known to Pharaoh through Moses — "Let my people go"
      • It was a command to repent
      • It was supported by God's awesome power
      • Pharaoh said "No"
    • God gave Pharaoh great abilities
      • Pharaoh knew it was wicked to oppress the people of God
      • God gave Pharaoh strength of mind and will for greater rebellion
      • Pharaoh should have used those gifts for righteousness
  • God Is Not in a Struggle with Pharaoh — evil
    • The outcome is never in doubt
    • God and His purposes are never in jeopardy
    • God is not just stronger than Pharaoh — God is God

    • All creatures must confess that He is God " Phl 2:10-11
