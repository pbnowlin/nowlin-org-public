• The Witness in Corinth – Act 18:1-17
    • The city of Corinth
      • A political center – Capital of Achaia
      • A commercial center
      • A pagan center
        • The temple of Aphrodite, goddess of sex
        • The mystery religions
      • A wicked center
        • Prostitution in the religions
        • Homosexuality in the city
        • The problem of drunkenness
    • Paul’s new friends – Act 18:1-4
      • Aquila and Priscilla
        • They were Jewish
        • They had to leave Rome due to anti-Semitism under Claudius
      • They were tentmakers
        • They were faithful workers
        • They helped the Apostle Paul
    • The ministry of Paul – Act 18:4-11
      • In the Synagogue
        • He reasoned with them
        • He persuaded them
        • He appealed to mind and to will
      • The arrival of Silas and Timothy
      • The stubborn resistance of the Jews – Act 18:6
      • The turning to the Gentiles – Act 18:6
      • The new converts – Act 18:7-8
        • They heard
        • They believed
        • They were baptized
      • The new encouragement – Act 18:9-10
        • The promised presence
        • The promised protection
        • The promised people
          • The sovereignty of God in salvation
          • The sovereign election - “I have many people in this city”
      • The response to the Lord – Act 18:11
        • Paul continued preaching
        • He stayed a year and a half
    • The attempt to silence the witness – Act 18:12-17
      • The occasion – Act 18:12 - A new pro-consul came to Corinth
      • The accusation – Act 18:13 - That Paul persuaded men to worship God contrary to the Law
      • The failure of their attack – Act 18:14-16
        • Gallio threw the case out of court
        • He ruled that it was a Jewish religious matter and not a Roman concern
      • The rage of the Jews - they beat their own leader, Sosthenes
  • Observations and Applications
    • Paul wrote his first epistles from Corinth - 1 and 2 Thessalonians
    • The decision of Gallio gave freedom to preach the Gospel throughout the Roman Empire without being charged with breaking the Roman law
    • Sosthenes became a believer – 1Co 1:1
    • From Athens to Corinth, from intellectual pride to sensual lust
    • The message proclaimed – 1Co 2:1-5
    • The church in Corinth
