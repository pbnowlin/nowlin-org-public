• The Events in Acts 12
    • The murder of James the Apostle by Herod – Act 12:1-2
    • The imprisonment of Peter – Act 12:3-5
    • The deliverance of Peter – Act 12:6-11
    • The prayer meeting – Act 12:12
    • The death of Herod – Act 12:20-23
  • The Murder of James
    • The murderer: Herod
      • He is known as Herod Agrippa
        • Not the one before whom Jesus appeared
        • The father of the Herod before whom Paul will later appear
      • He is opposed to Christ
      • He is a cunning politician
    • The victim: James – Act 12:2
      • He is the brother of John
      • He is the son of Zebedee
      • He was on the Mount of Transfiguration – Mat 17:1
      • He was the first Apostle to die
    • The execution
      • His head was chopped off with a sword – Act 12:2
      • It was commanded by Herod
      • It pleased the Jews
    • The timing
      • The year - 44 AD
      • The Passover season - Spring time
  • The Imprisonment of Peter – Act 12:3-11
    • Herod commands the arrest of Peter
      • A matter of political gain
      • A strategic time – Easter / Passover
    • Peter is secured in prison
      • He is chained to two soldiers on guard
      • Special guards are assigned to around the clock duty – Act 5:19
    • Peter is sleeping in prison
      • He has been there before
      • He is not afraid
      • Yet he is surprised
  • The Supernatural Deliverance of Peter – Act 12:7-11
    • The angel of the Lord appears
    • He is in charge
      • The guards are helpless
      • The chains are no problem
    • He directs Peter
      • Tells him everything to do
      • Helps him out of the prison
  • The Very “Human” Church – Act 12:13-17
    • Peter at the door
    • Rhoda answers the knock
      • Rhoda’s problem
      • The cautious recognition
    • The answered prayer
  • The Death of Herod – Act 12:21-23
    • The execution of guards
    • The pride of man - playing God
    • The weakness of man - worshiping Herod
    • The sovereignty of God
      • Herod dies a horrible death
      • The folly of those who defy God
      • The tragedy of those who give God no glory
      • The blinded, distorted, twisted, pride of men who attempt to live without a sense of dependence upon God
  • The Marvelous Results – Act 12:24-25
    • The Word of God grew and multiplied
      • Despite all the opposition
      • Paul and Barnabas are being trained for their future ministry
    • The certainty of justice
    • The indestructibility of the church
  • The Prayer Meeting – Act 12:5
    • James is murdered
    • Peter is delivered
    • The difference – Act 12:5
      • But prayer was made
        • The difference in weapons
          • Herod – sword
          • Church – 2Co 10:3-5
        • Prayer
          • To build dependence
          • To participate in the sovereignty of God
      • By the Church
        • Not by the government
        • Not by the world
        • The unique calling of the Church
      • Unto God
        • They called upon the Lord
        • They did not trust in anything or anyone else
      • Without ceasing
        • They were persistent
        • God delayed the answer
          • The people learned
          • Peter learned
      • For him Peter
        • The definite request
        • The direct answer
  • The Purpose of Recording the Events of Act 12:7
    • To reveal God’s sovereign determinism
      • Over Rome
      • Over Herod
      • Over Jews
      • Over persecution of the Church
    • To reveal God’s inclusion of His people in carrying out His purposes
  • God’s People Participate in His Sovereign Determinism
    • God’s predestined plan will be fulfilled
    • God’s plan includes the participation of His people
      • The prayer
      • The evangelism
      • The serving
    • Can Peter be released from prison if the church does not pray? Yes
      Will the Church pray? Yes, God wills it
    • God has willed to use the prayer of His people as the means to the end
    • Prayer – any participation by God’s people is our great privilege and opportunity and involvement with God
    • Since God determines everything, why pray?
      • Because He commands it
      • For our good
      • For our learning
      • For dependence
