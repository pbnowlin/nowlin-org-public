  • The Results of the Witness – Act 4:1-31
    • The first persecution – Act 4:1-7
      • The arrest of Peter and John
      • The priests, the captain of the temple and the Sadducees
        • They were outraged by the proclamation of the resurrection
        • Their leadership was threatened
        • They placed Peter and John in custody – Act 4:3
      • The great parenthesis
        • Some heard the Word of God
        • Some believed the Word of God
        • God made the difference, not man
        • The number of men was 5000 - how many women?
      • The trial
        • The Sanhedrin
          • The rulers, the elders, the scribes, the High Priest and all his relatives
          • The crucial question: “By what power, or by what name, have you done this?”
            • They were afraid
            • They were determined to stop it
              • Many governments today are opposed to this message
              • They are opposing it with all their might
    • Another witness to the Sanhedrin
      • To identify with Christ was certain death or imprisonment
      • The amazing courage displayed
      • The source of their courage - the indwelling Holy Spirit – Act 4:8
        • The first weakness among believers lies in not knowing our resources
        • The second weakness among believers lies in not believing in (counting on, relying upon) our resources
          • Others can tell us what to do
          • Only the Spirit of the risen Christ can empower us to do it
        • The evidence of courage – Act 4:10-11
          • Immediate exaltation of Christ
          • Direct accusation against them
          • Contrast Peter at the trial of Christ
    • The content of the witness – Act 4:10-12
      • The crucifixion of Christ
      • The resurrection of Christ
      • The continuing ministry of Christ
      • The identity of Christ – Act 4:11 – He is the Jewish Messiah
      • The salvation in Christ – Act 4:12
      • The message of Christ – Act 4:10 – to all the people of Israel
