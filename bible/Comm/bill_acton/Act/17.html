  • The Witness in Athens – Act 17:14-34
    • The city - famous for art, philosophy, and religions
    • The home of the Stoics and the Epicureans, Plato, Socrates, and Aristotle
      • The Stoics - Pantheists - followers of Zeno, believing that everything is God
      • The Epicureans - Atheists - denied God’s existence and anything after death
    • Paul’s message on Mars Hill – Act 17:16-34
      • The background
        • The philosophical debates
        • The pagan city
        • The stirring of Paul’s spirit to teach
      • The content – Act 17:22-31
        • The introduction
          • He commends their religious pursuit
          • He observes their altar
          • He identifies their “unknown god” (daimon)
        • The thesis – Act 17:22-29
          • The nature of God
          • God is Creator – Act 17:24-25
          • God is universally sovereign – Act 17:26
            • Does not dwell in buildings
            • Man cannot worship what man can make
            • God directs the destiny of men and nations
        • The application
          • An appeal to forsake idolatry – Act 17:29
          • A plea to repent – Act 17:30-31
            • The urgency – now
            • The imperative – God commands it
            • The universality – men everywhere
            • The reality
              • God has appointed a day of judgment
              • The standard of God’s judgment is righteousness
              • The righteousness demanded is the righteousness of Christ
              • The assurance is the fact of the resurrection of the Lord Jesus Christ
      • The result
        • Some mocked
        • Some wavered
        • Some believed
        • Paul departed
