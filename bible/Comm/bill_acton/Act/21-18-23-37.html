• Paul’s Witness in Jerusalem – Act 21:18-40; Act 22; Act 23:37
    • Before the mob – Act 21:18-40 - Act 22:23
    • Before the Sanhedrin – Act 22:30 - Act 23
  • Paul’s Witness in Caesarea – Act 23:12-26
    • Before Governor Felix – Act 24:1-27
      • The false accusers – Act 25:1-8
        • Ananias, the High Priest - filled with hatred
        • The Elders
        • Tentullus, the lawyer, a clever orator
      • The accusations – Act 25:2-9
        • Praise to Felix
        • Paul, a trouble-maker, a serious charge
        • Leader of a revolution, such as Barabas
        • Profaner of the Temple
      • The Defense – Act 25:10-21
        • The refutation of the accusations – Act 25:10-13
          • Denied the charges
          • Demanded proof
        • The statement of the real reason for their opposition – Act 25:14-21
      • The effect – Act 25:22-23
        • Felix is not impressed with the accusers
        • He postponed the case
    • The second witness before Felix – Act 25:24-26
      • The occasion: He wanted his wife, a Jew, to hear Paul
      • The content
        • Righteousness – Condemning the hypocrisy of the ruler
        • Temperance or self-control – Condemning the illegal marriage
        • Judgment
      • The result
        • Felix was terrified – Act 24:25
        • He kept Paul in custody
        • He tried to extort money
        • He committed spiritual suicide
        • Felix was a cruel ruler – Act 24:27
          • He was living in adultery
          • He was corrupt
          • He was a man of perpetual compromise
          • He was a man of procrastination
          • He was a man of vacillation
          • Vacillation becomes hardness
