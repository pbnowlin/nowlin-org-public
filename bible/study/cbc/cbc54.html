The Law of Moses

By the phrase "law of Moses" is meant all that was revealed through Moses. The New Testament treats it with great fullness and perpiscuity:

  1. Typical aspects. The law of Moses was only the shadow of good things to come (Col 2:17; Heb 10:1).

  2. Fulfillment. Jesus Christ declared His intention to fulfill every word of the law (Mat 5:17-18), and He did it (Luk 24:44).

  3. Its weakness. The law could not,

    1. bring about justification (Act 13:39);
    2. produce righteousness (Gal 2:21);
    3. produce life (Gal 3:21);
    4. bring about perfection (Heb 7:19);
    5. or free the conscience from a knowledge of sin (Heb 10:1-4).

  4. Impossible for all men to keep it. The law was given to and for Israel only (Exd 19:1-25; Exd 20:1-17; Mal 4:4; Jhn 1:1-17). Take two proofs of this:

    1. All the males of the Hebrews were commanded to appear before the Lord at a designated place three times a year (Exd 23:14-17; Exd 12:4-16);
    2. those to whom the law was given were commanded, on penalty of death, not to kindle a fire throughout their habitation on the sabbath day (Exd 35:1-3).

  5. Abolishment of the law. It is declared,

    1. that the law is abolished (2Cr 3:6-13; Eph 2:15);
    2. that Christ is the end of the law (Rom 10:4);
    3. that it was the ministration of death (Exd 32:1-28), and that it is "done away" (2Cr 3:7);
    4. that Jesus took away the first that He might establish the second (Heb 10:5-9);
    5. that it was nailed to the cross (Col 2:14-16);
    6. that those who had been under it had been delivered from it (Rom 7:6);
    7. that they were dead to it (Rom 7:4);
    8. that they were not under the law, but under grace (Rom 6:14);
    9. that they were no longer under the schoolmaster (Gal 3:24-25);
    10. that they were not required to serve the law (Act 15:1-24; Gal 3:19);
    11. that the Christian who sought justification under the law had fallen from grace (Gal 5:4);
    12. and that now the righteousness of God is revealed without the aid of the law (Rom 3:21-22).

  6. Contrasted with the gospel.

    1. The law was intended for one nation—Israel (Exd 20:1-17; Mal 4:4); the gospel of Christ is intended for the whole creation (Mat 28:18-20; Mar 16:15-16).
    2. The first covenant was dedicated with the blood of animals (Exd 24:6-8), the new covenant was dedicated with the blood of Jesus Christ (1Pe 1:18-19).
    3. The first institution was administered by frail men—the Levites (Lev 16:1-34; Heb 7:11-23); the second is administered by Jesus Christ, who was made priest, not by carnal commandment, but "after the power of an endless life" (Heb 7:16).
    4. Circumcision in the flesh was a sign of the first (Gen 17:1-14; Lev 12:3); circumcision in the heart and spirit is the sign of the second (Rom 2:25).
    5. The law of Moses guaranteed to the obedient Hebrews temporal blessings (Deu 28:1-6); the gospel of Christ guarantees spiritual blessings to those who live up to its requirements (1Pe 1:4).
    6. The law of Moses guaranteed to the Hebrews the land of Canaan (Deu 30:5-10); the gospel guarantees eternal life beyond the grave to those who honor the Lord (1Jo 5:20).
    7. The law of Moses required obedience to the one true God (Exd 20:1-5); the gospel emphasizes the Fatherhood of God (Mat 6:9).
    8. The law of Moses prohibited the people from taking the name of the Lord in vain (Exd 20:7); the gospel requires that our communications be "yea" and "nay", declaring that everything beyond is evil (Mat 5:37).
    9. The law of Moses required the Hebrews to remember the Sabbath day (Exd 20:8-11); in apostolic times, the people of God remembered the Savior in the feast that He ordained (Mat 26:26-30; 1Cr 11:23-29) on the first day of the week (Act 20:7).
    10. The law of Moses required children to honor their parents (Exd 20:12); the gospel requires children to obey their parents in the Lord (Eph 6:1-4).
    11. The law of Moses prohibited murder (Exd 20:13); the gospel prohibits hatred (1Jo 3:15).
    12. The law of Moses forbade adultery (Exd 20:14); the gospel prohibits even lust (Mat 5:28).
    13. The law of Moses prohibits stealing (Exd 20:15); the gospel prohibits stealing and requires benevolence (Eph 4:28).
    14. The law of Moses forbade the bearing of false witness (Exd 20:16); the gospel requires us to speak the truth in love (Eph 4:15).
    15. The law of Moses prohibited covetousness (Exd 20:17); the gospel requires us to do good unto men according to our opportunities (Gal 6:10), and love our neighbors as ourselves (Rom 13:10).
