Condensed Biblical Cyclopedia
Moses, the Man of God

  1. Date of his birth and death. Moses was born in Egypt two thousand four hundred and thirty-three years after the creation of Adam:

    1. It was (130 + 105 + 90 + 70 + 65 + 162 + 65 + 187 + 182 = 1056) one thousand and fifty-six years from the creation of Adam to Noah (Gen 5:3-29).

    2. It was (500 + 100 + 2 = 602) six hundred and two years from the birth of Noah to the birth of Arphaxad (Gen 5:32; Gen 7:6; Gen 11:10).

    3. It was (35 + 30 + 34 + 30 + 32 + 30 + 29 = 220) two hundred and twenty years from the birth of Arphaxad to the birth of Terah (Gen 11:12-24).

    4. It was (130) one hundred and thirty years from the birth of Terah to the birth of Abram (Gen 11:32; Gen 12:1-4; Act 7:1-4).

    5. It was (100) one hundred years from the birth of Abram to the birth of Isaac (Gen 21:5).

    6. It was (60) sixty years from the birth of Isaac to the birth of Jacob (Gen 25:26).

    7. It was (91) ninety-one years from the birth of Jacob to the birth of Joseph (Gen 41:46, '53-54'; Gen 45:4-6; Gen 47:9).

    8. It was (110) one hundred and ten years from the birth of Joseph to his death (Gen 50:26).

    9. It was (64) sixty-four years from the death of Joseph to the birth of Moses (Gen 12:1-4; Gen 21:5; Gen 25:26; Gen 41:46, '53-54'; Gen 45:6; Gen 47:9; Gen 50:26; Exd 7:7; Exd 12:40-41; Gal 3:17). (1056 + 602 + 220 + 130 + 100 + 60 + 91 + 110 + 64 = 2433). Add to this the (120) one hundred and twenty years that Moses lived (Deu 34:7), and you will have the date of his death.

  2. First forty years of his life. The distinguishing incidents of this period of his life were,

    1. he was concealed by his mother (Exd 2:1-2);

    2. he was placed in an ark of bulrushes (Exd 2:3);

    3. he was discovered by Pharaoh's daughter and named Moses because he was drawn out of the water (Exd 2:3-10);

    4. he was adopted by Pharaoh's daughter and educated (Exd 2:9-10; Act 7:20-22);

    5. he refused to be called the son of Pharaoh's daughter (Heb 11:24);

    6. he visited his brethren (Act 7:23);

    7. he slew an Egyptian (Exd 2:11-12; Act 7:24-25);

    8. his brethren failed to understand him (Exd 2:14; Act 7:22-28);

    9. he fled to Midian (Exd 2:15; Act 7:29).

  3. Second forty years of his life. The distinguishing incidents of this period of his life were,

    1. he met the daughters of Jethro, the priest of Midian, at the well and watered their flocks (Exd 2:16-17);

    2. he was invited into Jethro's house (Exd 2:17-20);

    3. he married Zipporah (Exd 2:21);

    4. the birth of his son Gershom (Exd 2:22);

    5. the death of the king of Egypt (Exd 2:23-25);

    6. and the appearance to him of the angel of God in the burning bush at Mount Horeb (Exd 3:1-16; Act 7:30).

  4. Third forty years of his life. The distinguishing incidents of this period of his life were,

    1. he was commissioned as the deliverer of his down-trodden brethren (Exd 2:7-22; Exd 3:1-6; Act 7:31-35);

    2. he departed for Egypt accompanied by his family, with the benediction of Jethro (Exd 4:18-20);

    3. the Lord appeared to him and emphasized his commission (Exd 4:21-23);

    4. he met and saluted his brother Aaron (Exd 4:27);

    5. they laid their commission before their brethren and received their recognition (Exd 4:29-31);

    6. Pharaoh refused to release the Hebrews (Exd 5:1-4);

    7. the magicians acknowledged the power of God (Exd 8:16-19);

    8. he became great in the estimation of the Egyptians (Exd 11:3);

    9. the passover was instituted (Exd 12:1-29);

    10. Pharaoh gave his consent to the departure of the people (Exd 12:31-36);

    11. the miracle at the Red Sea (Exd 14:9-22);

    12. the destruction of the Egyptians (Exd 14:23-31);

    13. the song of triumph (Exd 15:1-19);

    14. the miracle at Marah (Exd 15:23-26);

    15. the falling of the manna (Exd 16:1-15);

    16. the keeping of a Sabbath (Exd 16:16-35);

    17. the miracle at Rephidim (Exd 17:1-7);

    18. the war with Amalek (Exd 17:8-16);

    19. the visit of Jethro and the return of his family (Exd 18:1-6);

    20. he accepted Jethro's counsel and organized a judiciary (Exd 18:13-26);

    21. the arrival at Sinai (Exd 19:1);

    22. the Lord's covenant with the people (Exd 19:3-8);

    23. the proclamation of the ten commandments (Exd 20:1-17);

    24. the ascent into the mount where he spent forty days and nights (Exd 24:1-18);

    25. he received the pattern for the tabernacle (Exd 25:1-40);

    26. he destroyed the idolatrous worship inaugurated by Aaron (Exd 32:1-33);

    27. he set up his tabernacle or tent apart from the people (Exd 33:1-11);

    28. he beheld the passing glory of the Lord (Exd 33:12-23);

    29. the second visit into the mount and the renewal of the tables of stone (Exd 34:1-28);

    30. the shining of his face (Exd 34:29-35);

    31. the request for material to build the tabernacle (Exd 35:1-35);

    32. the setting up of the tabernacle (Exd 40:1-38);

    33. he acted as priest at the consecration of Aaron and his sons (Lev 8:1-36);

    34. he numbered the people (Num 1:1-46);

    35. the consecration of the Levites (Num 8:1-26);

    36. the second passover (Num 9:1-15);

    37. the departure from Sinai (Num 10:11-13);

    38. seventy elders were appointed to assist him (Num 11:16-30);

    39. he was antagonized by Miriam and Aaron (Num 12:1-13);

    40. he sent twelve spies into Canaan (Num 13:1-16);

    41. the rebellion in the camp, and the sentence of death passed by the Lord (Num 14:1-35);

    42. the rebellion of Korah (Num 16:1-40);

    43. the plague in the camp of Israel (Num 16:41-50);

    44. the budding of Aaron's rod (Num 17:1-13);

    45. he sinned at Meribah (Num 20:1-13);

    46. the death of Aaron (Num 20:22-29);

    47. his brethren were bitten by fiery serpents (Num 21:1-9);

    48. the conquest of the Amorites (Num 21:21-35);

    49. he was antagonized by Balak and Balaam (Num 22:1-41; Num 23:1-30; Num 24:1-25);

    50. whoredom and idolatry in the camp of Israel (Num 25:1-15);

    51. the second enumeration (Num 26:1-65);

    52. the appointment of the successor (Num 27:15-23);

    53. war with the Midianites (Num 31:1-54);

    54. he gave the Reubenites, the Gadites and the half-tribe of Manasseh the privilege of taking up their abode on the east side of the Jordan (Num 32:1-42);

    55. he wrote the journeyings of the children of Israel (Num 33:1-49);

    56. he described the borders of the land of promise (Num 34:1-29);

    57. he rehearsed the law in the plains of Moab (Deu 1:1-5);

    58. he emphasized the law in reference to vows (Deu 23:21-23);

    59. he begged the Lord to allow him to enter into the land of promise, but his request was refused (Deu 3:21-28);

    60. he finished recording the law and deposited the book in the ark of the covenant (Deu 31:24-26);

    61. he composed his last song (Deu 32:1-44);

    62. he gave his parting benediction to the tribes (Deu 33:1-29);

    63. he ascended to the summit of Pisgah, and beheld the land promised to Abraham, Isaac and Jacob (Deu 34:1-4).

  5. Leader. He stood at the head of the Hebrew nation as its leader for forty years (Exd 7:7; Deu 29:1-5; Deu 34:1-7; Acts 7:30-36).

  6. Priest. He acted as priest during the temporary worship at Mount Sinai (Exd 24:1-8), and at the consecration of Aaron and his sons (Lev 8:1-30).

  7. Poet. He composed,

    1. the song of triumph when they crossed the sea (Exd 15:1-19),

    2. a song of gratitude in the wilderness (Num 21:15-18),

    3. his farewell song (Deu 32:1-44).

  8. His character. Moses' character was many-sided. There are incidents in his life that exhibit every trait of the high, the pure, the noble:

    1. His refusal to be called the son of Pharaoh's daughter and his choice to suffer affliction with the people of God establishes the fact that he greatly loved his brethren (Exd 2:11-12; Heb 11:23-27);

    2. his attempt to be excused from the leadership of his people proves that he was a man of humbleness of mind (Exd 3:7-22; Exd 4:1-13);

    3. his address to the people at the Red Sea, while the Egyptians were sweeping down upon them, establishes the fact that he was a man of extraordinary faith (Exd 14:13-18);

    4. his sitting to hear all the complaints of Israel and passing judgment upon them from morning until night proves that he possessed great perseverance (Exd 18:13);

    5. his acceptance of the advice of his father-in-law in reference to the government of the people establishes the fact that he was progressive (Exd 18:17-27);

    6. his conduct at Mount Sinai in the destruction of the golden calf and its worshippers proves that he was aggressive (Exd 32:19-28);

    7. his fervent petition to the Lord to spare Israel even when God promised, on their destruction, to make of him a great nation proves his utter unselfishness (Exd 32:9-13);

    8. his refusal to rebuke Eldad and Medad for prophesying proves that he was free from envy (Num 11:27-29);

    9. his prayer to the Lord to forgive his sister Miriam when she had grievously sinned against him proves that he was meek and forgiving in an eminent degree (Num 12:1-13);

    10. his conduct in suppressing the insurrection inaugurated by Korah establishes the fact that he was a man of extraordinary courage (Num 16:1-40).

  9. His great opportunities. Moses enjoyed immense advantages,

    1. he was learned in all the wisdom of the Egyptians (Act 7:22);

    2. he received a commission directly from Jehovah (Exd 3:7-12);

    3. he enjoyed the privilege of talking with the Lord from face to face (Num 12:6-8) during his sojourn for eighty days and nights in Mount Sinai (Exd 24:12-18; Exd 31:18; Exd 34:1-28);

    4. he had a glimpse of the personality of Jehovah (Exd 33:12-23);

    5. he had no successor as a prophet (Deu 34:10);

    6. he is compared to the Messiah only (Deu 18:15, 18; Act 3:22).

  10. Messianic prophecies. There are ten passages in the writings of Moses that refer to Jesus Christ (Gen 3:14-15; Gen 12:1-3; Gen 18:18-19; Gen 22:15-18; Gen 26:1-5; Gen 28:10-15; Gen 49:8-10; Num 24:17; Deu 18:15, Deu 18:18).

  11. His disobedience. Moses was honored above all his contemporaries, and in many respects stands above every generation, and yet God shut him out of the land of promise on the account of one sin (Num 20:1-13).

  12. Important observation on his disobedience. Beware!

  13. Historian. He not only gave the law, but he gave the history of his people and the history of the world, in brief, from the beginning. That he received the revelations that are attributed to him, there can be no doubt.

    1. Over and over again it is asserted that "the Lord spake unto Moses";

    2. the book of Joshua endorses him (Jos 1:7);

    3. his book was in existence in the days of Josiah (Deu 31:24-26; 2Ki 22:1-11);

    4. he was recognized in the days of Malachi (Mal 4:4);

    5. and John the apostle asserts that the law came by Moses (Jhn 1:17).

  14. Sources of his information. Apart from the fact that Moses had direct communication with the Lord, it is easy to see that he could have received the history of the early ages through only a few persons:

    1. Adam was (130 + 105 + 90 + 70 + 65 + 162 + 65 = 687) six hundred and eighty-seven years old at the birth of Methuselah (Gen 5:3-21).

    2. Adam lived (930) nine hundred and thirty years (Gen 5:5), or (930 - 687 = 248) two hundred and forty-three years after the birth of Methuselah.

    3. Methuselah lived (960) nine hundred and sixty-nine years (Gen 5:27), or (969 - 243 = 726) seven hundred and twenty-six years after the death of Adam.

    4. Adam and Methuselah were contemporary (969 - 726 = 243) two hundred and forty-three years.

    5. Again, Methuselah was (187 + 182 + 500 = 869) eight hundred and sixty-nine years old at the birth of Shem (Gen 5:25-32).

    6. Methuselah lived (969) nine hundred and sixty-nine years (Gen 5:27), or (969 - 869 = 100) one hundred years after the birth of Shem.

    7. Shem lived (602) six hundred and two years (Gen 11:10-11), or (602 - 100 = 502) five hundred and two years after the death of Methuselah and Shem were contemporary one hundred years.

    8. Again, Shem was (102 + 35 + 30 + 34 + 30 + 32 + 30 + 29 + 205 + 25 = 552) five hundred and fifty-two years old at the birth of Isaac (Gen 11:10-32; Gen 12:1-14; Gen 21:5).

    9. Shem lived (602) six hundred and two years (Gen 11:10-11), or (602 - 552 = 50) fifty years after the birth of Isaac.

    10. Isaac lived (180) one hundred and eighty years (Gen 35:28), or (180 - 50 = 130) one hundred and thirty years after the death of Shem.

    11. Shem and Isaac were contemporary (180 - 130 = 50) fifty years.

    12. Again, Isaac was (60 + 91 = 151) one hundred and fifty-one years old at the birth of Joseph (Gen 25:26; Gen 41:46-54; Gen 45:6-11; Gen 47:9).

    13. Isaac lived (180) one hundred and eighty years (Gen 35:28), or (180 - 151 = 29) twenty-nine years after the birth of Joseph.

    14. Joseph lived (110) one hundred and ten years (Gen 50:26), or (110 - 29 = 81) eighty-one years after the death of Isaac.

    15. Isaac and Joseph were contemporary (110 - 91 = 29) twenty-nine years.

    16. It was only sixty-four years from the death of Joseph to the birth of Moses, and probably many of those who knew Joseph were alive in the days of Moses (Gen 12:14; Gen 21:5; Gen 25:26; Gen 41:46; Gen 41:53-54; Gen 45:4-6; Gen 47:9; Gen 50:26; Exd 7:7; Exd 12:40-41; Gal 3:17). Here is the chain:

      1. Methuselah was contemporary with Adam two hundred and forty-three years;

      2. Shem was contemporary with Methuselah one hundred years;

      3. Isaac was contemporary with Shem fifty years;

      4. Joseph was contemporary with Isaac twenty-nine years.

  15. His death. He died on Mount Pisgah, and was buried by the Lord in a valley in the land of Moab, and his grave was forever hidden from human sight (Deu 34:1-7).

  16. Entrance into the promised land. Prohibited from entering the promised land with his brethren on the account of sin, he at last stood upon the mountain in the beloved country in company with Elijah, Peter, James, John and the Lord Jesus (Mat 17:1-13).
