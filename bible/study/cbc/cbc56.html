The Kingdom or Church of Christ
  1. Prophecies concerning it.

    1. Isaiah and Micah predicted that the law should go forth out of Zion and the word of the Lord from Jerusalem (Isa 2:1-3; Mic 4:1-3);

    2. Jeremiah predicted that the Lord would establish a new covenant with the house of Israel and the house of Judah, that it should not be like unto the covenant that He made with their fathers when he brought them out of Egypt, that He would put His laws in their minds and write them in their hearts, that they would know the Lord and that their sins and iniquities should be remembered against them no more (Jer 31:31-34; Heb 8:7-13);

    3. and Daniel predicted that the God of heaven would set up a kingdom that should never be destroyed and that it should consume and break in pieces all other kingdoms, and stand forever (Dan 2:44).

    >
  2. Foundation. Isaiah predicted that a foundation stone should be laid in Zion (Isa 28:16), and that stone is Christ (Mat 16:13-20; Rom 9:32-33; 1Cr 3:10-11; Eph 2:19-22; 1Pe 1:6-8).

  3. Head. Jesus Christ is the head of the Church (Col 1:18 Eph 5:23).

  4. The beginning. In contemplating this subject, we should note the general drift of the Bible teaching prior to the death of Christ:

    1. It was in the future when God made promises to Abraham (Gen 12:1-3), to Isaac (Gen 26:1-5); to Jacob (Gen 28:10-14);

    2. it was in the future when Jacob prophesied of the coming of Shiloh (Gen 49:1, 8-10; Heb 2:14; Rev 1:1-5);

    3. it was in the future when Moses predicted the coming of One whose authority should be supreme (Deu 18:15-18);

    4. it was in the future when Isaiah predicted the bringing in of the Gentiles (Isa 54:1-3; Isa 62:1-4);

    5. it was in the future when John the Baptist preached in the wilderness of Judea (Mat 3:1-13);

    6. it was in the future when Jesus announced to His disciples that some of them would live to see it come with power (Mar 9:1);

    7. it was in the future when Jesus visited Caesarea Philippi (Mat 16:13-17);

    8. it was in the future, near the close of the earthly life of Jesus, for the disciples were expecting it to immediately appear (Luk 19:11-27);

    9. it was in the future when Jesus was on the cross (Luk 23:42-43);

    10. it was in the future after the death of Jesus on the cross (Mar 15:43);

    11. it was in the future, just preceding the ascension (Act 1:6-7).

    12. Previous to Pentecost the Church or Kingdom is spoken of as in the future, but after that day it is spoken of as having an actual existence (Act 2:41; Act 5:11; Act 8:1; Heb 12:28). It was necessary to abolish the first institution in order to establish the second (Heb 10:9): but the first was not removed during the life of Jesus, for He commanded His disciples to follow the teaching of the law as expounded by the Scribes and Pharisees (Mat 23:1-3), and the vail of the temple was not rent in twain until He expired (Mat 27:51; Eph 2:13-16).

    13. The new institution was to be characterized by the absolute blotting out of sins (Jer 31:31-34), and as the blood of animal sacrifices could never take away sin, it was indispensably necessary for Christ to die before the work could be done (Mat 26:28; Rom 5:9; Heb 9:14-22; Heb 10:4; 1Pe 1:18-19).

    14. The church was purchased by Jesus and was not His until He paid the price (Mat 20:28; 1Cr 6:19-20).

    15. The body could not exist without the spirit (1Cr 6:19-20; Jam 2:26) and the Spirit was not given until the glorification of the Lord (Jhn 7:38-39; Act 1:5).

    16. The prophets, Jesus Christ and His apostles agree in placing the beginning at Jerusalem (Psa 110:1-4; Isa 2:1-3; Isa 62:1-2; Joe 2:28-32; Mic 4:1-2; Luk 24:45-53; Act 1:5-8; Act 2:1-47; Act 8:1; Gal 4:21-31).

    The work was inaugurated on the first Pentecost after the ascension of Christ. This day is distinguished from all others in the world's history:

    1. The disciples received their first message from Jesus after His departure (Luk 24:50-53; Act 2:36);
    2. the Holy Spirit came into the world and began through the apostles the evangelization of the world (Jhn 14:16-18; Jhn 16:7-11; Act 2:1-4);
    3. the prophecies were fulfilled on that day (Isa 2:1-3; Psa 110:1-4; Joe 2:28-32; Mic 4:1-2; Act 2:1-47);
    4. Peter and the other apostles began to bind and loose in the name of Jesus (Mat 16:18; Mat 18:18; Act 2:37-38);
    5. the apostles proclaimed a law of remission in the name of Jesus (Mat 28:18-20; Act 2:37);
    6. the apostles did their first preaching under the last and great commission (Mar 16:15-16; Act 2:14-36).

  5. The Law of Admission. In general terms, the law of admission into the Church is the gospel, but conditions are specifically laid down in it by the Lord and His inspired apostles. The gospel may be divided and studied,

    1. in promise (Gen 12:1-3; Gal 3:16);
    2. in prophecy (Gen 49:10; Isa 7:14);
    3. in preparation (Isa 40:1-8; Mat 3:1-12);
    4. and in fact (Mar 4:26-28; 1Cr 15:1-3).

    These conditions to the alien are:
    1. Faith,

      1. its importance (Heb 11:6);
      2. it is taking God at His word (Rom 4:21);
      3. its unity (Eph 4:5-13);
      4. its basis is Jesus Christ (Jhn 8:24; 1Cr 3:10-11);
      5. it is produced by hearing the gospel (Jhn 20:30-31; Rom 10:17);
      6. it purifies the heart (Act 15:9);
      7. and its effect on the life (Jam 2:17-26).

    2. Repentance,

      1. God is willing for men to repent (Eze 18:25-32; 2Pe 3:9);
      2. men can repent, for salvation depends on it (Luk 13:1-5);
      3. the motives that produce it are the goodness of God (Rom 2:4), and the fear of judgment (Act 17:30-31; 2Cr 7:10);
      4. it is a change of mind resulting in a change or reformation of life (Isa 55:7; Isa 7:5; Mat 3:7; Jam 3:7-10).

    3. Confession,

      1. men are required to confess Christ (Mat 10:32-33);
      2. it is done with the mouth unto salvation (Rom 10:9-10);
      3. and in the presence of witnesses (Jhn 12:42; Act 19:18; 1Ti 6:12-14).

    4. Baptism,

      1. the subject—believers in Christ;
      2. the action—a burial;
      3. the design—for the remission of sins (Mat 3:14-17; Mat 28:18-20; Mar 16:15-16; Jhn 3:5; Act 2:38; Rom 6:1-3; 1Cr 12:13; Gal 3:26-27; Eph 4:5; Eph 5:26; Col 2:12; Heb 10:22; 1Pe 3:21).

  6. The life. Every member of the body of Christ is required to live righteously, soberly and godly in the present world (Tts 2:11-14), adding to his faith courage, knowledge, temperance, patient, godliness, brotherly kindness, charity (2Pe 1:5-7).

  7. The assembly. The ancient Christians assembled on the first day of the week to,
    1. break bread (Act 20:7);
    2. and contribute to the Lord's cause as He had prospered them (1Cr 16:1-2).

  8. Name of the Church. In New Testament times the Church was called the Church of Christ (Rom 16:16) or the Church of God (1Cr 1:1-2);

  9. Names of the members. The members of the Church of Christ were called, individually,
    1. saints (Rom 1:7);
    2. children of God (Rom 8:16);
    3. heirs of God (Rom 8:16);
    4. brethren (Rom 12:1);
    5. sons of God (1Jo 3:2);
    6. disciples;
    7. and Christians (Act 11:26; 1Pe 4:16).

  10. Early history. The early history of the Church is the history of triumph. The apostles began their labors in Jerusalem and in a very short time a very great number of people had yielded to the demands of Christ in Jerusalem and the surrounding country (Act 2:37-42 Act 4:1-4; Act 5:14; Act 6:7). Soon Philip, the evangelist, introduced the gospels in the city of Samaria with great success (Act 8:1-25), and Peter introduced it among the Gentiles at the house of Cornelius (Act 10:1-48; Act 11:1-26).
