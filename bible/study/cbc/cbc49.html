Condensed Biblical Cyclopedia
Kingdom of Israel
  1. Jeroboam, the first king. During Solomon's building operations he discovered a young man by the name of Jeroboam, the son of Nebat an Ephrathite, who was valorous and industrious, and he set him over the charge of the house of Joseph (1Ki 11:26-29). Subsequently as the young man was going out of Jerusalem, he was met by the prophet Ahijah who, in a very impressive manner, assured him that he should reign over ten of the tribes of Israel (1Ki 11:29-39). Solomon on hearing of this attempted to kill Jeroboam, and for protection he fled to Egypt (1Ki 11:40). Upon the accession of Rehoboam to the throne the people sent for Jeroboam, and he joined his countrymen in requesting the new king to lighten their burdens which he emphatically refused to do, and Jeroboam led the revolt (1Ki 11:1-24; 2Ch 10:1-19). Jeroboam established himself at Shechem in Mount Ephraim, and in order to prevent the people from going to Jerusalem to worship, set up two golden calves, one at Bethel and the other at Dan, assuring the people that these were the gods that had brought them out of the land of Egypt (1Ki 12:25-30). He also disregarded the law of God and made priests of the lowest of the people, and changed the time of holding the annual feasts ordained by Moses (1Ki 12:31-33). During these perilous times a prophet of the Lord from Judah went to Bethel and found Jeroboam officiating at the altar. The prophet cried vehemently against the altar and predicted that a child should be born to the house of David, Josiah by name, who would destroy the priests of this altar on account of their sacrilegious work, and emphasized the authenticity of his commission by causing the altar to open and the ashes to pour out. Jeroboam was greatly angered and attempted to arrest the man of God with disastrous results, but through the intercession of the prophet he was restored (1Ki 13:1-32). After this Jeroboam increased in wickedness (1Ki 13:33-34). Jeroboam's son Abijah fell sick, and he sent his wife to Shiloh to interview the prophet Ahijah in order to find out the destiny of the child. He told her that the child would die, and predicted the extinction of Jeroboam's house on account of his unparalleled wickedness (1Ki 14:1-18). Jeroboam reigned twenty-two years (1Ki 14:19-20). He reigned contemporaneously with Rehoboam seventeen years (1Ki 12:1-20; 1Ki 14:20; 2Ch 13:20), Abijah three years (1Ki 14:31, 1Ki 15:1-2), and with Asa two years (1Ki 14:20, 31; 1Ki 15:1-2, 8-10; 2Ch 12:13).

  2. Important fact—Ahijah the prophet. The prophet Ahijah flourished during the reign of Jeroboam (1Ki 14:1-18).

  3. Nadab, the second king. Jeroboam was succeeded by his son Nadab, whose uneventful reign continued only two years (1Ki 15:25).

  4. Baasha, the third king—second dynasty. Nadab was overthrown and succeeded by Baasha, who, as soon as he reached the throne, exterminated the house of Jeroboam because of his extreme wickedness (1Ki 15:2-30). Baasha walked in the footsteps of Jeroboam (1Ki 15:34). He was visited by the prophet of the Lord who predicted the destruction of his house on account of his sins (1Ki 16:1-7). Baasha reigned over all Israel twenty-four years (1Ki 15:34). He reigned contemporaneously with Asa (1Ki 15:9-10, 33).

  5. Important fact—Jehu the prophet. The prophet Jehu flourished during the reign of Baasha (1Ki 16:1-4).

  6. Elah, the fourth king. Baasha was succeeded by his son Elah, who reigned two years contemporaneously with Asa, king of Judah (1Ki 15:9-10; 1Ki 16:6-8).

  7. Zimri, the fifth king—third dynasty. Elah was assassinated by his servant Zimri who, as soon as he ascended the throne, destroyed all the house of Baasha according to the word of the Lord. Zimri reigned contemporaneously with Asa seven days (1Ki 15:9-10; 1Ki 16:8-30).

  8. Omri, the sixth king—fourth dynasty. Zimri was succeeded by Omri. He reigned six years in undisputed authority. He was contemporary with Asa (1Ki 15:9-10; 1Ki 16:21-23). The chief act of Omri's reign, was the founding of the city of Samaria (1Ki 16:23-24). His reign was characterized by evil (1Ki 16:25-27).

  9. Ahab, the seventh king. Omri was succeeded by his son Ahab (1Ki 16:28). He introduced idolatry into the court of Israel, and his reign was distinguished by its remarkable disregard for the law of God (1Ki 16:9-34; 1Ki 17:24). He reigned contemporaneously with Asa four years (1Ki 15:9-10; 1Ki 16:29) and Jehoshaphat eighteen years (1Ki 22:41-42).

  10. Important facts—Micaiah and Elijah the prophets. During the reign of Ahab two distinguished prophets flourished.
    1. Micaiah's history is very brief. Ahab formed a military alliance with Jehoshaphat, and they went to war against the king of Syria. Before they went into the battle, Ahab's prophets were called, and they uttered their predictions concerning the result of the contest, after which Micaiah was called, and in a very impressive manner predicted the result of the engagement, and his predictions were fulfilled (2Ch 18:1-34).
    2. Elijah is one of the most dramatic characters in history. Both his appearance and departure from the scenes of earthly conflict are most remarkable. He appeared at a time when idolatry held high carnival in the court of Ahab, and when all Israel had apparently departed from the Lord (1Ki 16:29-34; 1Ki 17:1).

    The chief events in life were,
    1. he appeared to Ahab, king of Israel and predicted that there should be neither rain nor dew except by his word (1Ki 17:1; Jam 5:17);
    2. he was fed by the ravens at the brook Cherith (1Ki 17:2-7);
    3. he arrived at Zarephath and dwelt there (1Ki 17:8-16);
    4. he restored the widow's son (1Ki 17:17-24);
    5. he appeared to Ahab the second time (1Ki 18:1-19);
    6. he repaired the altar of the Lord and destroyed the prophets of Baal (1Ki 18:20-40);
    7. the end of the drought and the race from Carmel to Jezreel (1Ki 18:41-46);
    8. he fled from the anger of Jezebel (1Ki 19:1-3);
    9. he sat down under a juniper tree and prayed for death (1Ki 19:4);
    10. the angel of the Lord appeared, fed, and strengthened him (1Ki 19:5-8);
    11. the Lord spoke to him at Mount Sinai and assured him that there were seven thousand in Israel who had not bowed their knees to Baal (1Ki 19:9-18);
    12. by the Lord's authority he anointed Elisha of Abelmeholah as his successor (1Ki 19:15-21);
    13. he predicted the terrible end of Ahab and his wife (1Ki 21:17-29);
    14. he called fire down from heaven (2Ki 1:1-12; Luk 9:54);
    15. he predicted the death of Ahaziah (2Ki 1:13-18);
    16. he was carried to heaven in a chariot of fire (2Ki 2:1-18).

  11. Ahaziah, the eighth king. Ahab was succeeded by his son Ahaziah. He followed in the footsteps of his wicked ancestors (1Ki 22:51-53). An accident befell him, and he was dangerously sick, and he sent to inquire of Baalzebub the god of Ekron if he would recover. The angel of the Lord commanded Elijah to go and tell the messengers to declare to the king that he should surely die. When the king recognized the prophet in their description, he sent a deputation of soldiers requesting him to come to him at once. Disaster followed disaster until the prophet appeared in the court of the king and predicted his speedy death (2Ki 1:1-16). Ahaziah reigned contemporaneously with Jehoshaphat two years (1Ki 22:42-51; 2Ki 3:1).

  12. Jehoram, the ninth king. Ahaziah was succeeded by his brother Jehoram (2Ki 1:17; 2Ki 3:1). His reign was characterized by evil (2Ki 3:1-2). The peace of his kingdom was disturbed by the rebellion of the king of Moab. In order to suppress this rebellion he associated with him the king of Judah and the king of Edom. Great destruction and sorrow followed (2Ki 3:1-27). He reigned contemporaneously with Jehoshaphat (2Ki 3:1), Jehoram (1Ki 22:42; 2Ki 3:1; 2Ki 9:29; 2Ch 21:1, 5) and Ahaziah (2Ki 9:29);

  13. Important fact—Elisha the prophet. Elisha the prophet flourished during these times. The chief events in his life were:
    1. he was anointed by Elijah as his successor (1Ki 19:19-21);
    2. he received a double portion of the spirit of Elijah (2Ki 2:9-15);
    3. he told king Jehoram how to obtain water during his campaign against the Moabites (2Ki 3:10-20);
    4. he increased the widow's oil (2Ki 4:1-7);
    5. he raised the Shunammite's son from the dead (2Ki 4:8-38);
    6. he performed a great miracle at Gilgal (2Ki 4:39-41);
    7. he fed a large multitude by a miraculous increase of the food (2Ki 4:42-44);
    8. he healed Naaman's leprosy (2Ki 5:1-19);
    9. he entailed leprosy on the house of Gehazi (2Ki 5:20-27);
    10. the great famine in Samaria (2Ki 6:1-7);
    11. he gave assistance to the king of Israel against his foes (2Ki 6:8-12);
    12. he was captured by the Syrians (2Ki 6:13-18);
    13. he led the Syrians to Samaria, fed them, furnished them with the necessities of life, sent them away, and thus gained a great victory (2Ki 6:19-24);
    14. he predicted sudden plenty, during the famine of Samaria (2Ki 7:1-2);
    15. his prediction was fulfilled (2Ki 7:3-20);
    16. he carried out the commission originally given to Elijah (1Ki 19:15-18; 2Ki 8:1-15; 2Ki 9:1-13).

  14. Jehu, the tenth king—fifth dynasty. Jehoram was slain and succeeded by Jehu, the son of Jehoshaphat, the son of Nimshi. He inaugurated a reformation by killing Jezebel, the sons of Ahab and the prophets of Baal (2Ki 9:1-37, 2Ki 10:1-28). Because of his success in the destruction of evil, the Lord promised him that his children should sit upon the throne for four generations (2Ki 10:29-34). Jehu reigned over Israel twenty-eight years, and was contemporary with Athaliah seven years (2Ki 10:36; 2Ki 11:1-4) and Jehoash twenty-one years (2Ki 12:1);

  15. Jehoahaz, the eleventh king. Jehu was succeeded by his son Jehoahaz, who reigned in Samaria seventeen years (2Ki 18:1). His reign was characterized by a continuance of the idolatrous practice inaugurated by Jeroboam. The anger of the Lord was kindled against Israel, and he delivered them into the hands of the Syrians. The king seemed to be penitent but did not reform (2Ki 12:2-8). He was contemporary with Jehoash seventeen years (2Ki 12:1; 2Ki 13:1).

  16. Joash, the twelfth king. Jehoahaz was succeeded by his son Joash, who followed in the footprints of his wicked progenitors. During Elisha's last illness he was visited by Joash to whom he communicated the information that he should smite the Syrians three times (2Ki 13:14-19). Joash reigned sixteen years, and was contemporary with Jehoash two years (2Ki 13:9-10; 2Ki 12:1; 2Ki 14:1) and Amaziah fourteen years (2Ki 14:1-2).

  17. Jeroboam II., the thirteenth king. Joash was succeeded by his son Jeroboam. He adhered to the ways of his ancestors. He restored the coast of Israel from the "entering of Hamath unto the sea of the plain," according to the prediction of Jonah the son of Amittai (2Ki 14:23-25). Israel was greatly afflicted during these times, but the Lord granted them deliverance by the hand of the king (2Ki 14:26-27). He reigned forty-one years, and was contemporary with Amaziah fifteen years (2Ki 14:1-2, 23) and Uzziah fourteen years (2Ki 15:1).

  18. Important fact—Jonah the prophet. Jonah the prophet flourished during the reign of Jeroboam the Second (2Ki 14:23-25).The chief events in his life were,
    1. he received a commission from the Lord to go unto the great city of Nineveh and cry against it (Jon 1:1-2);
    2. he was cast into the sea and swallowed by a great fish (Jon 1:3-17);
    3. he prayed to the Lord and was delivered (Jon 2:1-10);
    4. the people of Nineveh repented at his preaching (Jon 4:1-11).

  19. Interregnum. There was a space of about twenty-four years between the death of Jeroboam the Second, and the accession of Zachariah (2Ki 14:23; 2Ki 15:1, 8).

  20. Zachariah, the fourteenth king. Jeroboam the Second was succeeded by his son Zachariah in whom was fulfilled the promise of the Lord to Jehu (2Ki 14:29; 2Ki 15:8-12). He reigned six months contemporaneously with Uzziah (2Ki 15:1-2, 8).

  21. Shallum, the fifteenth king—sixth dynasty. Zachariah was slain and succeeded by Shallum who reigned a full month, contemporaneously with Uzziah (2Ki 15:1-2, 10, 13).

  22. Menahem, the sixteenth king—seventh dynasty. Shallum was slain and succeeded by Menahem. His reign was distinguished by a very great wickedness, war and excessive taxation (2Ki 15:14-22). He reigned ten years contemporaneously with Uzziah (2Ki 15:1-2, 17).

  23. Pekahiah, the seventeenth king. Menahem was succeeded by his son Pekahiah. His reign was distinguished on account of wickedness. He reigned two years contemporaneously with Uzziah (2Ki 15:1-2, 22-24).

  24. Pekah, the eighteenth king—eighth dynasty. Pekahiah was slain and succeeded by Pekah (2Ki 15:25-27). He departed not from the example of his progenitors (2Ki 15:28). He reigned twenty years (2Ki 15:27), and was contemporary with Uzziah about one year (2Ki 15:1-2), Jotham sixteen years (2Ki 15:32-33; 2Ki 16:1).

  25. Important fact—Interregnum. There was an interregnum of about eight years between the death of Pekah and the accession of Hoshea (2Ki 15:27; 2Ki 16:1-2; 2Ki 17:1).

  26. Hoshea, the nineteenth king—ninth dynasty. Pekah was slain and succeeded by Hoshea (2Ki 15:30). During the reign of Hoshea, Israel was carried by the Assyrians into captivity, and their country was occupied by their enemies (2Ki 17:1-41).
