Bible Versions
General

Questions frequently surface regarding the differences between different versions and translations, especially with the claim that there are “missing verses” in the more modern English versions. There are several works available that discuss the debate over the Greek sources of the New Testament. Therefore, we leave the reader to search out those sources for answers to this question, after much prayer for our Lord’s wisdom and guidance. We provide multiple versions for comparison purposes. Where possible, we provide the marginal note translation for the “missing verses”

In addition, required copyright information is provided for Bible versions as applicable.

American Standard Version (ASV)

Thomas Nelson & Sons first published the American Standard Version in 1901. This translation of the Bible is in the public domain.

Young’s Literal Translation (Young’s)

The Young’s Literal Translation was translated by Robert Young, who believed in a strictly literal translation of God’s word. This version is of the Bible is in the public domain.

The Darby Translation (Darby)

J. N. Darby translated the Bible in 1890. This translation of the Bible is in the public domain.

The Webster Bible (Webster's)

The Webster Bible was translated by Noah Webster in 1833 in order to bring the language of the Bible up to date. This version of the Bible is in the public domain.

Hebrew Names Version (HNV)

The Hebrew Names Version is based off the World English Bible, an update of the American Standard Version of 1901. This version of the Bible is in the public domain.

The Latin Vulgate (Vulgate)

The Latin Vulgate was translated from the original languages into Latin by Jerome in A.D. 405. For over a millennium, it remained as the preferred translation of the church. This version of the Bible is in the public domain.
